## [summary] Our reading of the month

This is the first issue of the Sinan monthly. It covers only two weeks (2 September to 15 September). We do not pretend two weeks stand for a month, but this cross-section is enough to see the structure of the market. Seven judgements follow, each backed by data from 1,803 relay sites and 52,000 effective quotes.

1. **The relay market is a discount channel for foreign closed models, not a model supermarket.** Foreign closed models (GPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok) sell at a market median of 15% of the official price; domestic models (DeepSeek, Qwen, GLM, Kimi, MiniMax) sell at a median of 97%, and half of them cost more than the official price. For domestic models, relays offer no price advantage.
2. **Price is not the competitive variable; the top-up rate is.** Of 353 sites with public quotes, half charge "1 yuan per 1 dollar of nominal credit", which prices foreign flagships at exactly 15% of official; another 30% charge close to real parity and land near 100%. The distribution is bimodal with almost nobody in between.
3. **48% of flagship quotes sit below the cost floor.** Under the assumption of compliant procurement with enterprise discounts of at most 50%, these prices do not add up. We do not speculate about any single site's upstream, but we list the three mechanisms that could explain the pattern, and the way to test them.
4. **There was no real price trend in the two weeks.** The chain-linked index level moved from 100 to 100.9, essentially flat. The jump in the discount ratio from 50% to 77% is entirely a change in sample composition, not a price move. A report that reads snapshots alone will reach the wrong conclusion.
5. **The relay premium on domestic models is converging.** Among price changes involving domestic models, 33 were cuts and 12 were rises; foreign models saw 24 cuts and 19 rises. Relay prices for domestic models are drifting back toward official prices. This is the hypothesis we most want to track.
6. **Subscription resale (the Sub2API family) now splits the market with pay-as-you-go.** 776 sites run the Sub2API panel, reselling seats of coding agents such as Claude Code and Codex; 85% have open registration and their prices sit behind a login. This is the largest structural change of 2026 in this market, and the hardest part to measure.
7. **Image and video reproduce the same dividing line.** Veo, Sora and Grok video sell below official prices on relays; Wan, Kling and Vidu sell above. Images behave the same way. Foreign cheap, domestic expensive, in the same direction as text.

## [scale] 1. Scale and structure: who sells, and how

**Two panels, two businesses.** The technical base of relay sites comes in two families. The one-api family (including forks such as new-api) counts 919 sites and bills by usage: the panel shows a nominal dollar price and users buy credit in yuan at a "top-up rate". The Sub2API family counts 776 sites and sells seats: monthly subscriptions to coding agents such as Claude Code and Codex are split and resold, with plan prices behind a login. The remaining hundred or so sites are self-built aggregators and sites exposing only an OpenAI-compatible endpoint.

**The rise of the Sub2API family is the most important structural fact of this issue.** The commodity is shifting from tokens to seats: buyers pay per month for concurrency and rate limits rather than per million tokens. These sites have open registration at 85% (659 of 776), higher than the one-api family (about 78% of sites we could classify), which says they are still expanding and competing for new users. We can measure their reachability and registration but not their prices yet; next issue we will read plan prices with registered accounts.

**Registration status is the market's "open for business" sign.** Of 1,803 sites, 1,062 have open registration, 201 are closed or invite-only, and 491 remain unclassified (mostly one-api sites with non-standard registration endpoints). A closed registration has two opposite meanings: "full, serving existing users only" or "about to shut down". We distinguish them by watching reachability and quotes over several days, and we do not conclude yet.

**Login methods reveal the community roots of the market.** 166 sites accept linux.do accounts (124 in the one-api family, 42 in Sub2API), the most concentrated third-party login after email and GitHub (332). linux.do is where the users gather and where new sites bootstrap traffic. 332 sites with GitHub login say the buyers are developers. Only 9 sites accept WeChat login: this market keeps a deliberate distance from China's mainstream identity systems.

**Domain choice shows how long operators expect to stay.** .com 530, .top 177, .ai 151, .cc 143, .xyz 104, and only 92 .cn. The budget suffixes .top, .cc and .xyz together exceed 80% of the .com count. A cheap domain does not indicate quality, but it does indicate expected lifetime and sunk cost. Only 300 sites (17%) hold an ICP filing, and that ratio is itself a judgement: most relay operators do not see themselves as companies intending long-term compliant operation in China.

**Sources of the index.** Of the 1,803 sites, 1,387 came from batch crawls of public directories, 161 from cross-referencing several sources, 66 from content fingerprinting (pages showing model names and prices from four or more vendors at once), 53 from the aiapirank and hvoyai directories, 64 from directories and code on GitHub, and 13 from links between sites. Along the way we removed 114 tool sites misclassified as relays and 3 vendor-owned domains. Next issue these numbers appear as increments rather than stock.

## [prices] 2. Prices: three tiers are three different markets

**How to read the chart.** Discount ratio = market median effective price ÷ official price. The market-wide 77% is the median across models and depends on which models are in the sample; the index level is chain-linked on models present on both days and reflects price alone. Over two weeks the level went from 100 to 100.9: prices did not move. The ratio went from 50% to 77% because on 5 September we indexed nearly 600 sites that charge close to dollar parity in one batch. **Almost all of the "price change" in these two weeks is us seeing more sites, not sites changing prices.**

**Foreign closed models: 15% is a convention, not the outcome of competition.** GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5, Claude Fable 5, GPT-5.6 Terra, Grok 4.6 and Claude Sonnet 5 all have a market median of exactly 15% of official. This is no coincidence: half of relay sites charge 1 yuan per dollar of nominal credit and copy the official dollar price as the nominal price; at 6.74 yuan to the dollar that is 14.8%. Of 303 sites selling foreign flagship or mid-tier models, 81 land exactly between 14% and 15.5%. The price is a tacit agreement among operators, not the result of each computing its own cost.

**Cost floor: 48% of flagship quotes do not add up.** For closed models every channel ultimately pays the vendor at API prices, and publicly documented enterprise discounts do not exceed 50%. Under that assumption, 48% of the 538 flagship quotes fall below the cost floor (under 15% of official) and another 16% sit between 15% and 40%; nearly two thirds are below what compliant procurement can explain. We do not speculate about any one site, but only three mechanisms can explain both "exactly 15%" and "persistent" at once: pooled subscription seats, where monthly plans such as Claude Code Max are split into API credit at near-zero marginal cost, which fits the rise of the Sub2API family; regional pricing and payment-channel differences; and sources we cannot verify. These three are indistinguishable by price. Only consistency probes and capability sampling can separate them, which is what we will add next issue (see part 3).

**Domestic models: relays charge more than the vendor, and the premium has a pattern.** The 13 domestic models sell at a median of 97% of official on relays, and five are above official: DeepSeek V4 Flash 869%, GLM-5.2 166%, DeepSeek V4 Pro 136%, GLM-5.3 Flash 123%, GLM-5.3 121%. This is not operator greed but a side effect of the panel: the one-api family prices each model as "multiplier × $2", multiplier tables mostly copy OpenAI's price structure, and the result is then multiplied by the 1-yuan-per-dollar top-up rate. For a model whose official price is $0.094 per million, that arithmetic lands far above the vendor. **The implication for buyers is direct: buy domestic open models from the vendor; relays offer no price advantage there. The entire price advantage of relays lies in foreign closed models.**

**The domestic premium is converging.** Of 45 confirmed price changes on domestic models (two consecutive fetches agreeing), 33 were cuts; foreign models saw 43 changes roughly balanced. The three largest declines in market median were Claude Sonnet 5 (−33%), GLM-5.2 (−20%) and Qwen 3.7 Max (−20%), the latter two being domestic models returning toward official. We list "domestic relay premium converging to within 110%" as the first hypothesis to watch next issue.

**Cross-site spread: the same model varies 1.3× across sites, and that is the value of comparing.** Taking for each model the 25th to 75th percentile range of effective prices divided by the median, then the median across models, the market-wide figure is 127%. Even after discarding the cheapest and most expensive quarter of sites, the remaining half still differ by 1.3× for the same model. That number answers "is it worth comparing" directly.

**Fast tier: 38% of quotes are far above official.** For models priced under $2 per million officially (GPT-5.6 Luna, DeepSeek V4 Flash, MiniMax M3 and others) the relay median is 91% of official, almost no discount, and 38% of quotes count as "far above public price". For these models relays sell convenience, one account and one bill for many models, not price.

## [reach] 3. Reachability and probes: what we measured, and what we could not

**Reachability separates sites between 99% and 99.9%, not between up and down.** Of the 234 sites above the threshold (≥24 probes in 7 days, ≥10 models on sale), 87 were reachable 100% of the time over 7 days, 86 between 99% and 99.9%, and 61 below 99%. Below 99% means more than 1.7 hours of unreachable API in a week. The three least stable, 32ai.uk (60.8%), bluebirdapi.com (67.5%) and llms.best (86.7%), are intermittent rather than offline.

**A mistake we must state plainly.** Between 18:00 on 11 September and 02:00 on 12 September our own probe node lost its network for 9 rounds, and all 1,801 sites were recorded as 0% reachable in those rounds. That pushed every site's 7-day reachability to about 93%, emptied the latency board, and made the first draft of this report say "0 sites at 100%". On 15 September we added a rule: when market-wide reachability in one round falls below 50%, the round is judged our fault and excluded from every site's account. The figures above are after that correction. The rule is in section 7 of the methodology.

**Consistency probes produced no valid sample this issue, and the fault is ours.** Consistency probes call each model with our own keys at each site and compare returned token counts. This issue we sent probes to 13 sites and 88 site-model pairs; all failed because the balance on our 7 keys ran out. Capability sampling produced 6 valid pairs, none below the cross-channel median. This means the report has no evidence on which of the three mechanisms in part 2 is at work, only prices. It is the largest gap in this report and we will close it next issue after topping up.

**Data audit: 5 released, 9 still on hold.** Of the 9 holds, 5 are "unit hints" (the panel text says per-second while we parsed per-call, mostly Grok and Kling video), 3 are "lone outliers" (more than half cheaper than the second-cheapest site in the family, with no peer), and 1 is a "board margin" case. Held quotes enter no comparison and no board.

## [media] 4. Image and video: the same dividing line

**Video.** Eight model families have an official reference price, with 817 comparable quotes. Family median effective price ÷ official reference: Sora (OpenAI) 97%, Grok Imagine video (xAI) 232%, Veo (Google) 445%; Wan (Alibaba) 333%, Kling (Kuaishou) 212%, Vidu (Shengshu) 167%, Hailuo (MiniMax) 297%, Seedance (ByteDance) 152%. The medians look high for both, but the distributions differ: of Veo's 166 comparable quotes, 115 are below the bulk-discount line; of Sora's 32, 20 are below the cost floor; of Wan's 191, 79 are above the public price. **Foreign video models on relays are "many cheap plus a few expensive"; domestic video models are "generally above official"**, exactly the split seen in text.

**Image.** Nano Banana (Google) 84%, Grok Imagine image 103%, Seedream (ByteDance) 91%, Qwen-Image (Alibaba) 139%, Wan image 260%, Kling image 24%. Kling image has only 25 quotes, 10 of them below the cost floor; we do not interpret it yet.

**Two holes still open.** GPT Image is on sale at 191 sites, the widest coverage of any image model, but it is priced per token and cannot be compared with per-image references, so it has no ratio this issue. HappyHorse, PixVerse and Midjourney together appear on 94 sites with no official per-second or per-image public price, and we do not use third-party prices as references. The holes themselves are an observation: the image and video products that relays sell most are precisely the ones whose prices are hardest to verify.

## [gpu] 5. The compute layer: domestic official prices already sit below the single-card cost line

**Card rental at period end.** H100 SXM: Vast.ai on-demand median $3.00 per hour, RunPod secure cloud $3.49; H200 $3.64 / $4.59; A100 80GB $0.72 / $1.59; RTX 4090 $0.47 / $0.74; RTX 5090 $0.54 / $0.99.

**Translated into token cost.** Under the assumptions in section 13 of the methodology (H100 at 3,000 tokens per second under high concurrency, 4090 at 800), running an open model on rented cards costs roughly $0.28 per million tokens (H100) or $0.16 (4090). Orders of magnitude only; the conclusion changes with throughput.

**That order of magnitude explains a pattern from part 2.** DeepSeek V4 Flash's official price is $0.094 per million tokens, below both cost lines; GLM-5.3 Flash at $0.25 sits near them. When a vendor's official price is already below the cost of renting a card, a relay has no room to undercut by self-hosting and can only sell at panel multipliers, which yields 869% and 123%. **By pushing official prices below the compute cost line, domestic vendors have squeezed the relay layer out of their own distribution.** That is a channel decision by the vendors; for buyers it means buy direct, for relays it means domestic models can only be a bundled extra.

## [implications] 6. What these numbers mean for different readers

We recommend no site. The following only translates measurements into facts each party has to face.

**Buyers.** If you buy foreign closed models, relays offer a 5× to 7× price difference, but the closer a price sits to the 15% line, the more you should ask where it comes from, because under compliant procurement it does not add up; consistency probes and capability sampling are checks you can run yourself, and our test page and command-line tool are free. If you buy domestic open models, buy from the vendor; relays have no price advantage and are usually more expensive. The same model varies 1.3× across sites, so ten minutes of comparison pays.

**Site operators.** Prices have converged; half of all sites sit on the same line, and price no longer differentiates. Two things do: verifiable stability (99.9% or better over 7 days, achieved by 173 sites this issue) and verifiable consistency. Our free verification turns both into a public mark; the more sites apply, the more "not applied" becomes a signal in itself.

**Model vendors.** Domestic vendors have priced so low that the relay layer cannot resell them, which places the whole burden of channel reach on the vendors themselves. Foreign vendors have a distribution layer in China they cannot see, priced at 15%, spanning 303 of the 1,803 sites.

**Researchers and press.** Cite the index level, not the discount-ratio snapshot; cite the pair "domestic 97% / foreign 15%" rather than the single "market 77%". The former is stable, the latter moves with the sample. The size of the Sub2API family is the fact most worth recording this issue; it appears in no public statistic.

## [watch] 7. What we will watch next issue

1. Whether the domestic relay premium converges to within 110% (median 97% this issue, five models above 120%).
2. After topping up probe keys, whether consistency probes differ systematically between sites priced at 15% and sites priced at 100%. This is the only way to separate the three mechanisms in part 2.
3. Sub2API plan prices, read after login, to build a second price table of "how much per month, how much concurrency".
4. The 30-day survival of the nearly 600 sites indexed in the 5 September batch, as a first estimate of this market's monthly churn.
5. Whether GPT Image's per-token pricing can be aligned with per-image references, bringing the most widely sold image model into the ratio.
6. Pass-through speed of official price changes: how many days relay medians take to follow a vendor repricing. No vendor repriced this issue, so it could not be measured.

## [method] Definitions and corrections

All figures come from the site's daily automated measurement: effective price = panel nominal price × top-up rate ÷ exchange rate; discount ratio = median effective price ÷ official reference; the index level is chain-linked with 2026-09-02 = 100. Domestic and foreign are classified by the vendor's registered location. Cost-floor assumptions are in section 13 of the methodology. Three method corrections were made this issue: exclusion of our own outage rounds (part 3); removal of 114 tool sites misclassified as relays and 3 vendor-owned domains; and a fix for video models billed per second that had been parsed as per call, retiring 144 old rows. Corrections are permanently public on the corrections page.
